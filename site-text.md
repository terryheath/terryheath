Help

Find your question below, or write to us with the form at the bottom.

Orders
Where is my order?

We email a confirmation when you pay. We mail print copies within a few days. Ebooks arrive by email right away, or on release day for a preorder. Nothing after a day? Check spam, then write to us with the email you used.

I can’t find my ebook download link.

Links work for 30 days and five downloads. Lost or expired? Write to us with the email you used, and we’ll send a new one.

Can I change or cancel an order?

Write to us right away. We can change a print order until it ships. We can’t cancel a delivered ebook.

Shipping
Where do print copies ship?

We ship print within the U.S.: $4 for the first copy, $1 for each after. Outside the U.S., buy the ebook. We email it anywhere.

I moved. How do I change my mailing address?

Sign in, open Account and update your address. We mail every subscription issue there.

Subscriptions
What do the subscriptions include?

Ebook, $20 a year: every issue as an ebook. Print, $48 a year: four printed issues mailed in the U.S., plus the ebooks. Add White Rabbit Ebook ($7) or White Rabbit Print ($14) to either.

How do I update my card, change plans or cancel?

Sign in, open Account and choose Manage billing. Update your card, see receipts, change plans or cancel there. We email you before each renewal.

My payment failed.

We didn’t charge you. Try another card from Subscriptions or Manage billing. Still failing? Write to us.

Contributor agreements and payments
How do I sign my agreement?

Open the link in your agreement email and sign online. It takes a minute. We email you a signed copy and keep it under Agreements in your account.

When and how am I paid?

We pay on the issue’s release day: Zelle or PayPal in the U.S., Wise elsewhere. Chose a subscription instead? It starts when you sign. See everything under Payments in your account.

How do I change my bio or headshot?

Sign in, open Account and edit your profile. The editor approves changes before they go live. Withdraw a change any time before then.

EPISODE 11

S. T. Joshi: H.P. Lovecraft, a Gateway to an Intellectual Life

S. T. JOSHI

Hosted by Terry Heath

 Listen to this episode · 52 min

At thirteen, in the public library in Muncie, Indiana, S. T. Joshi found H. P. Lovecraft and was, in his words, completely blown away. At sixteen he read him a third time with a dictionary beside him, having decided he needed to know exactly what words like eldritch meant if he was going to understand what Lovecraft was saying.

Fifty years of work have followed. Six years at Brown — a BA and an MA in classics rather than English, because Lovecraft’s papers were in the library there. A journal founded at twenty-one. Twenty-four volumes of letters, edited with David E. Schultz, completing the whole of Lovecraft’s surviving correspondence. A twelve-hundred-page biography. His own press, issuing roughly a title every two weeks.

The thread kept going. Lovecraft led him to Machen, Dunsany and Blackwood, then to Bierce, Mencken and George Sterling, then to the classics, colonial architecture, and a two-volume history of atheism in the West. He calls Lovecraft a gateway to his whole intellectual life, and here he follows it everywhere it has taken him — including, this month, the first full biography of Clark Ashton Smith.

About Inkhorn Press

Inkhorn Press is a nonprofit press. We publish Inkhorn Review, a quarterly of poetry, short prose and art, and White Rabbit, which appears every Friday the 13th. We also run events.

Who publishes what

Inkhorn Review was founded in 2026 by Terry Heath, its editor. Its Autumn 2026 and Winter 2026 issues are published by Follow the Rabbit LLC. From the Spring 2027 issue, Inkhorn Press publishes the Review. Gifts to Inkhorn Press support the press and its contributors.

How to read

Every piece is free to read online, with no subscription. Sign up for the newsletter and one piece arrives in your inbox every Sunday.

Buy print and ebook editions in the shop. A Print subscription mails you four issues a year in the U.S. and includes the ebooks. Outside the U.S., subscribe to the ebooks. We price print to cover printing and postage.

Your donations cover production and help us pay contributors more.

The editor

Terry Heath is a writer and teacher. He is founding editor of Inkhorn Review and host of the podcast Life on Words. His own poems appear in various journals. He teaches at Olympic College in Bremerton, Washington, and founded the BookFolk festival.

Submit

We read year-round. Submissions are $5, with free reading periods quarterly. See the submission guidelines.
